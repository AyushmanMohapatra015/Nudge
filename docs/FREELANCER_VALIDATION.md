# Freelancer validation guide

Use [VALIDATION_SESSION_KIT.md](VALIDATION_SESSION_KIT.md) for moderator task cards, a private worksheet, observation rules and evidence-based sponsor/product decisions. The kit is prepared; sessions and findings remain pending.

Status: prepared, not conducted. No findings, testimonials, or impact metrics have been collected. Aim for three independent freelance web designers or small agency owners who have completed a fixed-price client project recently. A small interview set gives direction, not statistically representative evidence.

## Arrange a conversation

The project owner conducts outreach; no messages have been sent by the coding agent. Suggested invitation, for the owner to send manually:

> Hi! I'm researching how freelance designers handle client requests that change after the project scope is agreed. Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation about a recent project? No confidential client details or documents are needed. This is research, not a sales call, and you're welcome to skip any question.

Before taking notes: explain that anonymized themes may inform a hackathon prototype and presentation; ask permission to use those notes. Default to written notes, no recording. Ask separate permission for any public quote. Do not request contracts, payment credentials, client names, screenshots of private messages, or real invoices.

## Interview: 15 minutes, before showing Nudge

1. **Context (2 minutes):** What kind of projects do you do? How was your most recent fixed-price project's scope agreed and approved?
2. **Actual change (4 minutes):** Tell me about the last request that arrived after scope agreement. What did the client ask for? What did you do next, step by step? If no change occurred, record that rather than forcing a story.
3. **Decision and evidence (3 minutes):** How did you decide whether it was included? What wording or history mattered? Was the difficult part identifying the change, discussing it, getting approval, or charging for it?
4. **Consequences (2 minutes):** Was extra work paid, absorbed, declined, or still unresolved? If you know roughly how much time it involved, what is your estimate? Keep estimates distinct from records.
5. **Payment and tools (2 minutes):** How did you get the changed work approved and invoiced? What tools did you use? What happened when payment was late? Do you already use PayPal, and would a PayPal-only prototype fit your workflow?
6. **Trust and priorities (2 minutes):** What would you never want software to decide or send without reviewing it? Which step would you most want to improve? What works well in your current process?

Avoid leading questions like “Would AI save you time?” or “Would you pay for Nudge?” Do not pitch during the incident walkthrough. Record disconfirming evidence, including users who have clear scopes and no chasing problem.

## Optional prototype walkthrough: separate 10 minutes

Use an isolated simulation workspace with fictional data; never show the owner's real sandbox records or private environment. Explain that AI and payment are simulated in this walkthrough. It tests usability, not real AI accuracy or live PayPal delivery.

Ask the participant to:

- Find what the client agreed to and where the new request differs.
- Explain which parts appear included, additional, or unclear, and what evidence they need.
- Review a proposed deliverable and price; identify which approval happens before invoicing.
- Explain an invoice's outstanding amount and a blocked reminder.

Ask them to think aloud; avoid coaching. Record completion, assistance, misunderstanding, and one suggested improvement. Do not claim scope correction works before it is implemented. Stop timing if you intervene and note the intervention.

## Synthesis and decision

After three conversations, compare concrete incidents and recurring steps. Keep counts tied to this small sample. For example, “2 of 3 interviewees described absorbing extra work” is only permitted if those notes exist; it is not a claim about all freelancers.

- Continue the scope-to-approved-payment focus if concrete change-approval incidents recur and the product addresses their difficult step.
- Reconsider the flow if users mainly need communication clarity, revision history, or different payment tools; do not simply add more features.
- If late payment dominates but scope ambiguity does not, revisit the emphasis using evidence before expanding collections features.
- Do not treat praise, willingness-to-try, or a hypothetical price as validated demand. Do not invent saved hours, conversion rates, revenue, or quotes.
