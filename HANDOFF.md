## Start here

Nudge is a PayPal AI Hackathon prototype for freelancers managing agreed project scope, extra-work requests, client decisions, and payment. Its differentiator is a traceable, human-controlled flow: AI can draft a scope assessment, but the owner reviews the evidence and sets any price; the client separately accepts or declines; only accepted work can lead to an invoice. Payment status is confirmed through PayPal data and webhook handling.

The project has extensive local implementation and test evidence, but it is **not submission-ready merely because the local suite passes**. Live website AI, a fresh-payment webhook transition, human usefulness, manual accessibility, sponsor/judge access, publication decisions, and deployment terms still have open gates.

## Product and original goals

The original hackathon requirements are central PayPal Developer Platform use in the free sandbox, meaningful AI, a working non-trivial prototype, enough documentation for judges, a public open-source GitHub repository with a visible licence, and a public YouTube demo under three minutes. The rules referenced by the project require a functioning demo through judging. Current project docs record an official November 12, 2026 submission deadline and December 1–15 judging; the owner’s earlier target is to have the prototype/video ready in the first week of November. Recheck the official rules before final submission.

Nudge is deliberately a single-owner freelancer prototype, not multi-tenant SaaS. Its main audience is a freelancer or small studio that needs to resolve scope changes without losing the evidence, price authority, client decision, and payment trail. It should address the social friction of explaining extra work, not just label a request with AI.

The product story should remain compact and demonstrable:

**Agreed scope → new request → clause evidence and uncertainty → owner review and price → client decision → PayPal invoice → payment status.**

Do not claim an AI assessment is legally binding, that a link proves the client’s identity, that payment itself accepts work, or that the system autonomously sends money. In this prototype, client-link possession records a decision; it is not a verified e-signature.

## Important decisions and reasons

1. **AI proposes; the owner decides.** A schema-valid AI result is still an unpriced draft. Ambiguity and clarification questions must remain visible; the owner confirms reviewed work and sets the price. This contains model errors and keeps responsibility clear.
2. **Client decision, invoice creation, sending, and payment are separate.** The client cannot change a price. Accepted work is required before invoice creation. PayPal’s current invoice state is authoritative; webhook payload state alone is not.
3. **Demo and sandbox evidence must not be conflated.** Simulation is useful for repeatable interaction, UI tests and video fallback, but it does not fulfill the requirement for meaningful PayPal and AI integration by itself.
4. **No forced sponsor additions.** AG Studio and Bryntum have concrete product roles. Other sponsor resources should be adopted only for a real user need, after checking the applicable allowance, rights, expiry, exhaustion behavior, and judge access. Sponsor use is a means to a stronger product, not a checklist.
5. **Spend and privacy are explicit constraints.** The owner is a student in India, has said to be careful at all stages, does not want unexpected charges, and is cost-conscious. Never ask for or print keys/passwords; never put them in prompts or docs. Keep AI calls bounded and disabled until an explicitly approved usage check. Do not use real client data in fictional demos.
6. **Slow, evidence-led development.** The owner repeatedly asked for a top-notch backend, small tracked tasks, exact status, and strict adherence to `REVIEW_ACTION_PLAN.md`. Update the action plan and top checkpoint docs when a task changes their state. Do not jump ahead just to add features.
7. **Git workflow.** The owner wants a reminder after each change to commit and push, but Codex must not commit or push unless explicitly authorized. Provide safe commands and distinguish new work from existing unstaged files.
